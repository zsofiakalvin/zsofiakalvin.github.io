AI Process Automation use cases

AI-driven automations from my own work in access management, GRC, support and delivery. Each one follows the same path: source data, an AI draft, a person who checks it, and a logged output.

Key considerations

Responsible AI principles I apply to every automation, based on my AAIR training and audit work.

Trust, but verify

AI output is treated as a draft. Results are checked against the source data before they are used.

Aligned with NIST AI RMF: Measure

Human oversight

Every automation has human oversight. If an output changes a system, a role or a decision, a named person approves it first. If it only informs, such as a report, the owner reviews it before acting on it.

Good practice based on EU AI Act Art. 14. A legal requirement only for high-risk AI.

Inputs and outputs are logged

Every input file and every output is kept, so a reviewer can see what went in and what came out.

Good practice based on EU AI Act Art. 12 and ISO/IEC 42001. A legal requirement only for high-risk AI.

Every output links to its source

In audit, evidence without a source does not count. Outputs keep a reference to the data they came from, so a reviewer can check them.

Aligned with NIST AI RMF: Govern, accountability and documentation

Data is protected

The automations run on Claude Enterprise, where company data is not used to train the model. Personal data is removed or pseudonymised before it reaches the model, and the security team configures the permissions of the MCP connections.

Aligned with GDPR Art. 25, data protection by design; ISO/IEC 27001 A.8.11, data masking

01 Testing and assuranceSAP access redesign UAT, built from real usageA test script per user, built from the transactions each person actually used.
Testing and assurance

SAP access redesign UAT, built from real usage

ProblemAn SAP access redesign project moved every user to new roles. They still needed to do their daily work as before, with no operational impact.

Outcome
Before
Test scripts were linked to each user's transactions and mapped to the process by hand, which took a lot of time.
After
Test scripts based on each user's actual usage and the process flows. Users moved to the new SAP roles without disruption to their daily work. Around 100 users tested across the global releases. About 80% of the generated scripts were correct first time, and the rest were corrected from tester feedback in each release.
Approach

For each tester, the system audit log showed which transactions they had used over a selected period, and their current roles were exported. An LLM linked those transactions to the documented process flows and wrote a test script per user. The steps follow the actual business process, and the expected outcome of each step is defined by that process.

The scripts were shared in SharePoint, with a dashboard to track progress.

02 GRC and access riskAI-assisted SoD rulesetAn LLM reviewed the documented process flows for SoD risks and critical actions, as the base for a custom ruleset.
GRC and access risk

AI-assisted SoD ruleset

ProblemThe business processes were documented in detail, but there were too many process flows to review manually for segregation of duties risks and to map to system requirements within the project time frame and with the available resources. The SoD ruleset was not clearly mapped to the business processes.

Outcome
Before
Manual review only, with an SoD ruleset that did not reflect the business processes.
After
A reviewed baseline SoD and critical access ruleset, ready to extend.
Approach

An LLM went through the process flows and, using defined keywords, flagged where SoD conflicts could occur, which role each step belongs to, and which actions are critical.

Process owners and subject matter experts reviewed the findings. Technical experts then mapped the processes to system transactions and authorization objects to build a custom SoD and critical access ruleset.

Limits

03 Operational risk signalsSupport ticket insight dashboardTurns escalated support tickets into insight on emerging risks and knowledge gaps.
Operational risk signals

Support ticket insight dashboard

ProblemSupport handles a large number of tickets, often routed across several teams. The goal was to find bottlenecks and recurring issues.

Outcome
Before
No visibility of where the knowledge base falls short or where tickets get stuck.
After
Recurring escalations became visible by category. Many were already covered by existing procedures, so those were shared and training was added where the process was unclear. These escalations went down afterwards. This was observed, not measured, and other changes in the same period may have played a part.
Approach

An automation collects the tickets first-line support could not resolve or needed help with. An LLM sorts them into predefined categories and suggests the root cause and the help that was needed.

The results feed two reports on a dashboard. One shows ticket volume and type to flag emerging risks. The other shows knowledge gaps behind escalations.

04 Identity and access management (IAM)Self-service role catalogueA searchable guide to system roles, written for business users and refreshed every week.
Identity and access management (IAM)

Self-service role catalogue

ProblemTechnical role names meant little to business users, so it was hard for them to know which access to request.

Outcome
Before
Business users chose access from technical role names.
After
One searchable page explains roles in business terms, used by business users and the support team.
Approach

Role data is extracted from the ERP system every week. An LLM turns it into a searchable page where people can find roles by transaction description, job title or business process, and see whether a role gives display or maintain access.

Each role shows what it contains, with best-practice guidance on which role to request. The support team uses the same page to answer access questions.

05 DeliveryAutomated project management and trackingTurned a written project plan into Jira tickets and a dashboard that stays in sync.
Delivery

Automated project management and tracking

ProblemThe project plan lived in a document and the work lived in Jira, so status had to be updated in two places.

Outcome
Before
Status was updated by hand in more than one place.
After
Status is kept in Jira only, and the dashboard is updated from it every week.
Approach

An LLM turned the written project plan into a dashboard and a set of Jira tickets, assigned to people and sprints.

A weekly scheduled task updates the dashboard from Jira.
