Use cases: AI governance

AI governance use cases

The following use cases show an example approach for an AI asset registry, and for building AI risk assessment into the change management lifecycle. An AI registry only stays current if new AI development is identified.

Key considerations

Principles I apply to AI governance, based on my risk and audit work and the frameworks behind it.

You cannot outsource risk

Buying an AI tool from a vendor does not hand over the risk. If something goes wrong, the company is still accountable.

Aligned with ISO/IEC 42001 A.10. For high-risk AI, also EU AI Act Art. 26, deployer obligations.

You cannot accept a risk you are not aware of

You can only accept a risk you know about. That is why the first step is to find every AI system and give it an owner.

Aligned with NIST AI RMF: Map; ISO/IEC 42001 6.1.2

Regulations keep changing

The AI Act was amended within two years of coming into force. Governance is reviewed continuously.

Aligned with ISO/IEC 42001 9.3 management review and 10 improvement

Built into existing processes

AI controls are built into processes people already use, such as change management and access reviews, instead of a separate process next to them.

Aligned with ISO/IEC 42001 5.1, integration into business processes

01 AI inventoryAI asset registryA questionnaire and IT interview to build an inventory of AI systems, with owner, data, access and AI Act risk category.
AI inventory

AI asset registry

ProblemThere is no complete inventory of AI assets. AI systems have no assigned owner, and it is unclear which ones process personal data, who has access to them, and which fall under the EU AI Act.

Outcome
Before
Unclear governance around AI assets.
After
An AI asset registry with an owner, data, access and AI Act risk category for every AI system, reviewed periodically.
Anonymised example
Approach

Each department fills in a short questionnaire: which AI tools they use, for what, with which data, and whether the output affects decisions about people. A structured interview with IT then checks the answers against SSO apps and procurement data, and covers what departments cannot see: who can configure each tool, which service accounts and API keys it runs on, and what other systems it can reach.

An LLM turns the answers into draft registry entries with an initial AI Act risk category and its rationale. The asset owner confirms each entry and the AI governance lead confirms the category. New AI systems are registered before go-live, and each row gets a review date: every 6 months for high-risk or internet-facing systems, every 12 months for the rest.

Standards and frameworks
ISO/IEC 42001 A.4.2–A.4.6 NIST AI RMF Map ISO/IEC 27001 A.5.9, A.8.3 GDPR Art. 30 AI Act Art. 3
Control
Human-in-the-loop: the LLM only drafts. The asset owner confirms each entry, the AI governance lead confirms the AI Act risk category, and high-risk or unclear cases go to legal.
Security
The registry holds sensitive information, such as service accounts, access rights and where API keys are stored, never the keys themselves. Access is limited to the AI governance team and asset owners, and changes are logged.
Traceability
Each row records who approved the system and when, with a link to the questionnaire answers, interview notes and evidence.
Limits
Completeness depends on what people declare. Free tools used outside SSO and procurement can be missed. The AI Act risk category is an initial assessment, not legal advice.
02 AI in the development lifecycleAI risk assessment in the change management processEvery new development is screened for EU AI Act impact, routed to the right approver and added to the registry.
AI in the development lifecycle

AI risk assessment in the change management process

ProblemNew AI development is not identified before go-live. AI features reach production without an AI risk assessment.

Outcome
Before
No AI risk assessment in the change process, and unclear approval responsibilities.
After
Every user story is screened during refinement, the right approver gets an approval ticket automatically, and approved AI changes are added to the registry.
Anonymised example
Approach

The screening is part of the agile cycle. During refinement, the product owner adds the AI screening form to the user story, and the developer fills it in before work starts. Power Automate reads the answers, picks the approver with fixed rules and opens an approval ticket.

Approval is part of the Definition of Done, so the story cannot be released without it. When an AI change is approved, it is added to the AI asset registry.

Standards and frameworks
ISO/IEC 42001 6.1.4, 8.4, A.5, A.6 NIST AI RMF Map ISO/IEC 27001 A.8.25, A.8.32
Control
Routing uses fixed rules, so who approves is predictable and auditable. Only the approver can approve.
Traceability
The answers, approver and decision are kept on the approval ticket and linked to the user story and the registry entry.
Limits
Screening relies on honest developer answers, so approvers spot-check them. Yes/no questions can miss edge cases, and unclear ones go to legal. This is a screening, not legal advice.
